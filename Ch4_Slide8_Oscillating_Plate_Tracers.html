Flow above an oscillating plate with vertical blowing

The plate at y = 0 slides back and forth; its motion diffuses upward and decays, while a uniform upward blowing carries fluid away from it. Drag the release point, scrub time, and compare the three tracer curves. Switch to the steady field to see them collapse onto one curve.

1 · Streamlines

Freeze time. Tangent to velocity: dx/dy = u/v = e−y cos(t − y). Integrate in y with t a parameter.

x(y) = x0 + F(y0) − F(y),  F(y) = ½ e−y [cos(t − y) + sin(t − y)]

A different picture at every instant. The family slides sideways as the plate reverses.

2 · Pathlines

Follow one particle released at (x0, y0) at t0. From v = 1, y = y0 + (t − t0), so t − y = t0 − y0 is constant along the path and dx/dt = e−y cos(t0 − y0).

x(t) = x0 + e−y0 cos(t0 − y0) [1 − e−(t − t0)]

Each particle keeps the phase it was born with; its sideways drift decays as it rises and saturates at x0 + e−y0 cos(t0 − y0).

3 · Streaklines

All particles that passed through (x0, y0) between t0 and now, each at its current position. Parameterize by release time τ.

y = y0 + (t − τ),  x = x0 + e−y0 cos(τ − y0) [1 − e−(t − τ)],  t0 ≤ τ ≤ t

The cosine in τ makes the dye line wave: alternate releases were flung left and right by the plate.

4 · Steady case

Drop the t: u = e−y cos y, v = 1. All three curves coincide.

x(y) = x0 + G(y) − G(y0),  G(y) = ½ e−y [sin y − cos y]

Same integral with t = 0 in the phase but no time dependence, so a particle that follows it, the instantaneous tangent curve, and the dye line are all the same curve.
